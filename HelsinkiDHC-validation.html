Helsinki region district heating model

Does the model reproduce 2016–2025?

The Backbone model of the district heating systems of Helsinki, Espoo and Vantaa is run for each year 2016–2025 on that year's plants, prices and weather. It is not told how the companies ran their plants. This page compares its yearly results with Finnish Energy's district heating statistics and the Energy Authority's verified CO2 emissions.

How to read a panel. Solid line: statistics. Dashed line: model. The number in a panel's corner is the mean difference over 2023–2025, model minus statistics: the years after the shaded 2020–2022 (COVID and the gas crisis), and the reference period for the future runs. Numbers in the text are over all ten years unless the text names other years. Differences are in GWh a year (CO2 in kt), not in percent: a small fuel can be far off in percent and matter little, and too much of one fuel beside too little of another is a wrong split, which the fuel bars show, not a wrong total. Hover a point for its value.

In short

  • The regional totals follow the statistics. Over the ten years total fuel use is −403 GWh a year from the reported 14,643, heat from CHP plants +1 from 6,468, CHP electricity +280 from 3,640 and CO2 −310 kt from 3,311. All four fall over the decade as the statistics do.
  • The split between fuels is the weak part, not the total. Natural gas is −1,173 GWh a year, coal +487, wood +494 and other fuels −245: opposite gaps that leave −403 in total. Most of the gas gap is from 2016–2021 (−2,131 a year); in 2023–2025 it is −333.
  • A city is further off than the region. Heat from CHP plants is −513 GWh a year in Helsinki, −72 in Espoo and +586 in Vantaa, which add up to +1. The model makes more of the region's heat in Vantaa and moves it to Helsinki: +218 GWh a year more than is reported bought from Vantaa.
  • 2023–2025, the years most like the future. The fuel split is closer: natural gas −333, coal −91, wood +280 and other fuels, mostly oil, −384 GWh a year. The largest gap left is CHP electricity: +672 GWh a year against a reported 1,265, and above the statistics in all three cities, with heat from CHP plants +471 and from heat-only boilers −515.

Much of this is bookkeeping. The statistics count heat that a CHP unit makes without electricity as heat-only production, and Vantaa reports its flue-gas heat recovery with heat pumps; in the model only the waste plant has such a bypass. So CHP plants +586, heat-only boilers −287 and electricity and recovery −154 GWh a year largely cancel. What is left is heat the model makes in Vantaa and sends to Helsinki.

The model moves more heat between the cities than is reported bought: +218 GWh a year more from Vantaa (+232 in 2023–2025), +58 from Espoo, and +126 from Helsinki in 2016–2018. In the model a link is limited by its capacity and a transfer cost. Spilled heat is heat produced and not used; it stands for the plants' auxiliary coolers, which are not modelled as units. Vantaa spills regularly from 2022 (74 GWh a year), where waste is burnt for electricity through the summer. In Helsinki and Espoo fuel costs more and the storages take up short surpluses, so spilling rarely pays: 51 and 17 GWh in 2022 and a few GWh in the other years.

How it was run. Each year is one run of 365 daily solves from 1 January. Every solve looks 28 days ahead, in hourly steps at first and daily steps at the end. Fuel and CO2 prices are one value a year, as in the runs of the future years; electricity prices and heat supply are hourly.

All 10 runs finished every one of their 365 daily solves and all 8,760 hours, with no dummy penalty and no warning.

Heat supply is input, not a result. The hourly supply the model is given (heat sold plus network losses) is −2.1% to +0.2% from the statistics, depending on the year (furthest in 2025), and the same in every city to within 0.1 percentage points. What the model produces above it is the spilled heat shown above.

The statistics. Finnish Energy's yearly district heating tables (heat production and use, heat bought between companies, fuels at lower heating value for whole plants) and the Energy Authority's verified emissions of each installation in the EU emissions trading system. Espoo is Fortum's Espoo network with Kauniainen and Kirkkonummi. Wood is wood chips, pellets and other biomass; other fuels are oil, bio-oil, peat and biogas. From 2025 the statistics book gas taken from the gas grid as biomethane in a column of its own; it is counted here as natural gas, as the model burns it.

Rebuild: python validation-scripts/build_validation.py from the model folder; its docstring names the steps before it.
